## 1. The observation (hypothesis generator only — never support)

Same individual as the dental-clamp and venipuncture material: in the past, being **stared at for a sustained period, or intently listened to**, could escalate to panic — even when the other person was attentive, not hostile. The symptom later declined on its own to mild nervousness, with no specific intervention.

Immediate-safety layer: no current emergency signal from this history (no syncope, chest pain, neurological deficit reported). Any real episode with loss of consciousness, chest pain, dyspnea, or arrhythmia is a medical-differential problem first. And this pattern must NOT be reflexively classed as specific phobia: fear of scrutiny sits closest to **social anxiety disorder (SAD)**, and panic attacks co-occurring with SAD do not imply panic disorder.

## 2. Three events side by side

| event | outward appearance | shared candidate structure |
|---|---|---|
| sustained gaze / intent listening | social attention | interaction continues, endpoint unknown |
| mouth held open at the dentist | procedural constraint | swallowing/speech/movement restricted, duration uncertain |
| venipuncture after a failed stick | medical procedure | "almost done" becomes "how many more times?" |

The triggers look unrelated; the candidate common quantity is not the needle, the mouth, or the gaze, but:

> **Loss of a predictable endpoint** + **reduced freedom to regulate while remaining engaged.**

Working phenotype (frozen wording, C32):
> **Panic emerges when continued engagement becomes temporally open-ended while engagement-preserving regulation feels increasingly unavailable.**

This is the Open-Door structure in the social domain: physical threat ≈ constant and low, escape availability = high — and panic occurs anyway. What contracts is the estimated **engagement-recoverability horizon** (êRRH^social): "I can leave — but I cannot keep *being in this* much longer."

## 3. The rival, given first-class standing (verified holdings)

The social-evaluation account — being observed → negative-evaluation threat → anxiety — is well armed and must get its chance to kill this extension. Field-verified anchors (abstract holdings unless stated):

- Singh JS, Capozzoli MC, Dodd MD, Hope DA. *The Effects of Social Anxiety and State Anxiety on Visual Attention: Testing the Vigilance-Avoidance Hypothesis.* Cogn Behav Ther, 2015. PMID 25767901.
- Seefeldt WL, Krämer M, Tuschen-Caffier B, Heinrichs N. *Hypervigilance and avoidance in visual attention in children with social phobia.* J Behav Ther Exp Psychiatry, 2014. PMID 24103693.
- Dong X, Gao C, Guo C, Li W. *Time course of attentional bias in social anxiety: The effects of spatial frequencies and individual threats.* Psychophysiology, 2020. PMID 32557636.
- Weiser LAM, Kersting A, Suslow T, Günther V. *Automatic avoidance of faces primed by threatening expressions in social anxiety: sustained but not early attention.* J Affect Disord, 2026. PMID 42025635.

The single-case detail the rival explains less naturally: **attentive listening — with no plausible negative evaluation — also triggered the response.** That is the crack C32's experiment widens into a dissociation.

## 4. The killer dissociation experiment (design frozen in C32)

Fix across ALL conditions: gaze intensity, evaluation threat, listener expression, physical distance, escape availability. Vary ONLY the engagement horizon:

| condition | endpoint | regulation affordance |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | known — "30 seconds", visible countdown | — |
| **B** | unknown — "they will keep listening" | — |
| **C** | unknown | **pause-and-continue**: "request a 5-s pause, then resume"; interaction survives |
| **D** | unknown | **escape-only**: one button, but pressing it ends the conversation |

C and D have the SAME number of control buttons — the difference is purely whether the correction preserves engagement.

**Frozen predictions (C32):**
- **P-IL1** (both theories; weakly diagnostic): B > A on anxiety.
- **P-IL2** (RRHM-specific, the sharp edge): **C > D** on maintained engagement and lower peak anxiety, despite equal control availability. The value is in *engagement-preserving* correction, not in control count.
- **P-IL3** (loads against the rival): under a pure social-evaluation account, A ≈ B at identical gaze and evaluation threat; a clear B > A difference at matched evaluation is evidence the horizon term is doing work.

**Kill criteria (frozen):** C ≈ D kills P-IL2 and with it the interactional-lock extension; A ≈ B kills the horizon term in the social domain. Prior guesses recorded in the prereg (P-IL1 0.85 / P-IL2 0.55 / P-IL3 0.6).

## 5. What this buys RRHM — and what it does not

It generalizes **engagement-preserving correction** beyond physiology to full organism–environment regulation (motor + autonomic + interoceptive + social + procedural) while keeping ONE variable, êRRH — no separate "social RRHM". Subtype state variables differ (dental: jaw/swallow/respiration/communication; BII: circulation/syncope susceptibility/nociception; social: gaze/speech/turn-taking/pause/disengagement), but the common quantity stays:

> **How long can I remain in this interaction while effective regulation remains available?**

The spontaneous decline of the symptom is *consistent with* natural recalibration (repeated benign exposure → êRRH↑ without fear = 0), matching RRHM's remission definition — single case, provenance only, zero evidential weight.

What it does not do: it does not show RRHM explains this better than SAD. Both accounts survive the anecdote; only the A–D experiment separates them. Until that experiment runs, this whole document is a registered hypothesis with kill criteria, nothing more.
